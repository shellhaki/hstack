# Direction: decide what this video looks and feels like

Every product is different, so every video must look different. The kit gives you techniques (springs,
transitions, timing to words); it does not give you a video. Before the storyboard, choose a
**direction** that could only belong to this product. A direction is four choices: a concept, a look, a
motion language and a structure.

## 1. Concept: one idea the whole video is built on
Find it in the product's own world, not in the kit. Ask:
- What object, place or ritual does the product replace or live next to? A secrets manager lives next
  to a vault, a keyring, a sealed envelope. A department site lives next to a noticeboard, a printed
  timetable, a lecture hall, a stamped memo.
- What does the product's own UI already look like? A timetable grid, a terminal, a map, a feed, a
  chart. The UI itself can become the world the camera moves through.
- What does the user feel before and after? Lost → oriented. Noisy → quiet. Slow → instant.

Write the concept in one line: "The video is one continuous camera move across a giant printed
timetable that turns into the website." "Every scene is a pinned note on a corkboard; the last one is
the site." A concept that would fit any product ("words slam in, then the logo") is not a concept.

## 2. Look: choose each part from the brand and the concept
| Choice | Options (pick, don't default) |
|---|---|
| Stage | brand color full-bleed · dark · paper · a texture from the concept (paper grain, corkboard, blueprint grid, screen glow) · the product UI itself as the background |
| Type | the product's own display font, large: headlines fill a third of the frame or more. Body text in a video is at least 40 px at 1080p |
| Color | the brand's colors, plus at most one accent from the concept. The brand color may be the background, the type or fills; check contrast |
| Framing | flat 2D graphic · 3D space with depth · close crops of real UI (a cropped part at 2–3× scale beats a whole screen at 1×) · split screen · one continuous shot |
| Texture | none · grain · halftone · paper · scanlines: only if the concept calls for it |

The product fills the frame. A whole app screen shown at real size is too small to read on a phone:
crop to the part that matters and scale it up.

## 3. Motion language: one way of moving, used consistently
Choose one and describe it in a line:
- **Elastic:** springs with overshoot, playful (consumer apps, social).
- **Mechanical:** linear slides, hard stops, snaps to a grid (developer tools, infrastructure).
- **Editorial:** slow eased moves, long holds, big type, few elements (serious, institutional).
- **Physical:** things fall, stack, pin, fold, with gravity and weight (anything with a physical
  metaphor: paper, cards, boards).
- **Continuous camera:** no scene cuts at all; the camera travels through one big world.
- **Organic product film:** the real product, driven by a cursor and typing, with depth (a huge
  blurred word behind small sharp text, far elements out of focus), slow dollies into the UI, small
  sounds on every interaction and a music bed the cuts land on. Calm, premium, believable. The default
  for product launches and feature announcements.
- **Hyper:** beat-locked kinetic graphics, a cut every beat or two, slams, flashes, split screens and
  a planned peak (`guides/hyper/SKILL.md`). For teasers, stingers and social cut-downs; never for
  explainers.

A film can open in hyper for 3–6 seconds and settle into an organic product film; say so in the
direction, and change language only at a clear boundary.

The transitions follow the language: an editorial video uses few, slow transitions; a continuous one
uses none, only camera moves.

## 4. Structure: choose the shape of the story
Pick the one that fits what the product does best (`guides/story/SKILL.md` describes each):
problem → fix · a day in the life · before / after · one continuous product journey · a question and
its answer · a list that builds · a manifesto.

## References the user sent
If the user sent videos or prompts they like, break them down first (`guides/references/SKILL.md`) and
let each direction say which techniques it borrows. Never their scenes, layouts, copy or characters.

## Write three directions, pick one
In `STORYBOARD.md`, under DIRECTIONS, write three directions that differ in **concept and structure**,
not only in color. Each is five lines: concept, look, motion language, structure, why it fits this
product. Recommend one. The frontier approves or picks another before any scene is planned.

## The defaults that make every video the same
None of these may appear unless the chosen direction explicitly calls for it, with a reason:
- Off-white paper with a dot grid and a colored glow at the bottom (the kit's old default stage).
- The name spinning in on slot reels after "Introducing".
- Generic chat windows filling with messages to show "the problem".
- Three words slammed one by one ("Copied. Pasted. Forgotten.").
- Giant rolling verbs with a command typed under each.
- A logo, a tag line and a URL pill as the last scene.
- Any line from the kit's sample script or an earlier video.

If your plan is close to this list, it is the kit's sample video, not a direction. Start again from
the concept.
