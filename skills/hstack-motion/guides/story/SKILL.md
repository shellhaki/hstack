# The story

A product video is short (15–30 s for a teaser, 30–60 s for a launch, 1–2 min for an explainer) and has
one job: make the viewer want to try the product. It works when every scene has one idea and the order
builds toward one moment.

## Structures (pick one in `guides/direction/SKILL.md`)
- **Problem → fix:** show the pain as a scene the viewer recognises, then the product removing it. Use
  it only when the pain is specific and visual. It's the most overused shape; prefer another if one
  fits.
- **A day in the life:** follow one person through moments where the product helps (8:00 the
  timetable changes, 10:00 the notice is already there…). Good for tools people use daily.
- **Before / after:** the same moment twice, split screen or in sequence. Good when the difference is
  instantly visible.
- **One continuous journey:** the camera travels through the product itself, screen to screen, without
  a cut. Good when the product is the selling point.
- **A question and its answer:** open on a question the viewer has asked themselves; the rest answers
  it. Good for products that remove a doubt.
- **A list that builds:** each scene adds one capability to a growing picture, which completes in
  the last shot. Good for platforms with several parts.
- **A manifesto:** a few strong statements about what the product believes, with the product as the
  proof. Good for brands with a clear point of view.

Every structure needs: a first second that hooks (motion and a clear subject, not a title card), one
moment that lands hardest, and an ending that says where to go. The ending is part of the concept, not
a generic logo card.

## Writing the script
- Write in the product's own voice and words: its README, landing copy and UI labels. If the product
  says "notices", don't say "news".
- Short sentences, written to be heard. Read every line aloud.
- Lists flow as one phrase ("courses, projects and staff"), not separate sentences.
- No stock phrases. Each of these needs a reason specific to this product, or it goes:
  "Introducing …", "Right now …", "… fixes that", "Copied. Pasted. Forgotten.", "One place for …",
  "Built for teams", "Meet …", "Say goodbye to …", "… made simple", "The future of …".
- A video can have no voice and no words for whole scenes. Let the images carry it where they can.
- Never downplay a competitor; never claim something unique that a competitor also has. Check their
  site.
- Every number on screen comes from the code or a dated source.

## Pacing
- Vary scene lengths: 1–2 s for quick beats, 4–6 s for the moment that lands. The voice decides the
  length of a narrated scene (`guides/voice/SKILL.md`).
- Nothing waits long after the voice ends (0.3 s tail).
- Vary the rhythm: a fast run, then one held shot. Equal-length scenes feel like a slideshow.
